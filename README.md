## Introduction

Indigenous Studies is an interdisciplinary field centered on the histories, governance, philosophies, and living realities of Indigenous peoples from their own intellectual traditions. Rather than examining Indigenous communities as historical artifacts or peripheral subjects within colonial narratives, the discipline centers inherent sovereignty, land-based epistemologies, and persistent resistance to settler colonialism. Studying this field challenges settled assumptions about law, ecology, community, and nationhood, offering critical tools to analyze how colonial institutions continue to operate and how Indigenous nations sustain distinct ways of being.

This curriculum is intended for self-directed learners with no prior formal background in the discipline. It requires no specialized training in law, political science, or anthropology. The primary requirement is an openness to critical self-reflection, an unlearning of conventional national narratives, and a commitment to engaging directly with Indigenous scholars, knowledge keepers, and legal thinkers on their own terms.

### How to Move Through the Curriculum

The curriculum is structured into five sequential subjects designed to build conceptual literacy before tackling complex legal and political debates:

Start with **Introduction to Indigenous Studies** to build a firm grasp of essential terminology, legal classifications, and community diversity while dismantling common public misconceptions. 

Move next to **Settler Colonialism and Indigenous Histories** to understand colonization as an ongoing structural process of dispossession rather than an isolated historical event, examining its operation across northern and southern Turtle Island (Canada and the United States).

Once this structural context is established, explore **Indigenous Knowledge Systems and Epistemologies** alongside **Indigenous Sovereignty, Law, and Governance**. These two areas articulate positive Indigenous alternatives to Western paradigms, demonstrating how relational ways of knowing, Traditional Ecological Knowledge, and inherent legal orders function independently of state recognition.

Finally, bring these historical, philosophical, and legal frameworks together in **Contemporary Indigenous Issues and Decolonization**, which examines present-day state violence, grassroots resistance, and the practical demands of land repatriation and cultural resurgence.

### Scope

This page contains only the foundational core that every learner in the discipline should understand. It deliberately emphasizes North American (Turtle Island) contexts, which serve as the standard theoretical anchor for introductory Indigenous Studies. 

Once you have completed this foundational grounding, you can branch into specialized fields—such as environmental defense, health systems, tribal governance, or visual sovereignty—in [Advanced Topics](advanced_topics.md).

## Curriculum

### Introduction to Indigenous Studies

This subject introduces foundational concepts of Indigeneity, distinct worldviews, key terminology, and the core frameworks that govern Indigenous-settler relations.

[Indigenous Canada (University of Alberta / Coursera)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/indigenous-canada) - A 12-module introductory open-access course that serves as the visual and lecture anchor for the discipline, surveying historical encounters, governance, and contemporary arts and culture.

[Indigenous Writes: A Guide to First Nations, Métis & Inuit Issues in Canada (Chelsea Vowel)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781553796800) - Designed to be read alongside the Indigenous Canada course, this companion volume unpacks essential terminology, explains legal designations, and systematically dismantles prevalent colonial myths.

### Settler Colonialism and Indigenous Histories

This subject examines how settler colonialism operates as an ongoing structure of dispossession and forced assimilation rather than a past event, highlighting the persistent resistance of Native nations.

[Histories of Indigenous Peoples and Canada (John Douglas Belshaw, Sarah Nickel, and Chelsea Horton / TRU Open Learning)](https://histindigenouspeoples.pressbooks.tru.ca/) - A comprehensive open-access textbook providing structured historical coverage from pre-contact confederacies and treaty periods to residential schools and political mobilization across northern Turtle Island.

[An Indigenous Peoples' History of the United States (Roxanne Dunbar-Ortiz)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780807057834) - A geographical and conceptual complement to the Belshaw text that analyzes how US military policy, westward expansion, and legal regimes systematically dispossessed Indigenous nations south of the 49th parallel.

### Indigenous Knowledge Systems and Epistemologies

This subject explores Indigenous philosophical paradigms, focusing on relationality, Traditional Ecological Knowledge, land-based pedagogies, and epistemologies distinct from Western scientific frameworks.

[Aboriginal Worldviews and Education (University of Toronto / Coursera)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/aboriginal-education) - A structured online course that establishes the theoretical groundwork in Indigenous ways of knowing, storytelling methodologies, and relational pedagogies.

[Braiding Sweetgrass: Indigenous Wisdom, Scientific Knowledge and the Teachings of Plants (Robin Wall Kimmerer)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781571313560) - Builds directly upon the concepts in the Coursera course through an accessible, narrative examination of Traditional Ecological Knowledge and reciprocal plant-human relationships.

### Indigenous Sovereignty, Law, and Governance

This subject covers inherent self-determination, distinct Indigenous legal traditions, treaty federalism, and the political struggles of Indigenous nations within and against colonial constitutional frameworks.

[Canada's Indigenous Constitution (John Borrows)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781442610385) - A foundational legal treatise that articulates the sources and scopes of Indigenous legal traditions, including sacred, natural, deliberative, and customary legal orders in relation to state law.

[Land Back: A Yellowhead Institute Red Paper (Yellowhead Institute)](https://redpaper.yellowheadinstitute.org/) - An open-access policy report that applies Borrows' legal theories to modern frontline struggles, detailing how contemporary Crown consent regimes and injunctions dispossess Indigenous nations and how communities reassert jurisdiction.

### Contemporary Indigenous Issues and Decolonization

This subject analyzes ongoing systemic inequities, frontline grassroots resistance, and the practical application of decolonial resurgence and grounded normativity.

[Decolonization is not a metaphor (Eve Tuck and K. Wayne Yang)](https://jps.library.utoronto.ca/index.php/des/article/view/18630) - A seminal open-access essay establishing that genuine decolonization requires the concrete repatriation of Indigenous land and life rather than superficial institutional reconciliation.

[As We Have Always Done: Indigenous Freedom through Radical Resistance (Leanne Betasamosake Simpson)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781517903879) - Extends the decolonial principles of Tuck and Yang by articulating how place-based practices, grounded normativity, and community resurgence operate outside the colonial politics of state recognition.

[Seven Fallen Feathers: Racism, Death, and Hard Truths in a Northern City (Tanya Talaga)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781487002268) - Serves as an investigative case study that grounds the systemic state neglect and institutional racism analyzed by Simpson, Tuck, and Yang in the lived experiences of Indigenous youth.
